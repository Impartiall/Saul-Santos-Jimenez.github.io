EK131 · Spring 2026

Arduino Temperature Monitor

A battery-powered, standalone room temperature monitor built from scratch over one semester. Uses a TMP36 analog sensor, a 10-bit ADC, and an I2C LCD to display real-time temperature in Fahrenheit. Triggers a buzzer and blinking red LED alarm when temperature leaves the 62–75°F range.

Arduino Uno C++ Onshape CAD 3D Printing KVL Soldering 22 AWG wiring

What I learned

The gap between a circuit on paper and one that works reliably inside an enclosure is where most of the real engineering happens. Tolerances, wire routing, heat shrink insulation, and troubleshooting short circuits taught me more than any single lecture did.

EPIC Lab · Summer 2026

D-Shaft Coupler for ME359

Designed and machined a D-shaft coupler for an electric motor, built for ME359 — a new course being developed at BU after seniors were reaching capstone without knowing how to fabricate their own prototypes. The course has students design, build, and test a full transmission against a professor's target, with only the housing and motor held constant.

Wire EDM Onshape CAD Transition fit Precision tolerancing

What I learned

The coupler needed a transition fit tight enough to slide onto the motor shaft without play, but I hadn't taken any coursework on fit tolerances yet. I researched transition fits on my own, designed the part in Onshape, and added a 0.003" offset to get it right. I also had a single day to finish it on the wire EDM — one of the most advanced machines in the shop — which meant making a real call under pressure about how to approach the cut, and how to work through a disagreement with a coworker about the best way to get there.

BURPG · In Progress — Fall 2026

Hybrid Intro Project (HIP) — Rocket Nozzle & Test Computer

Designing a rocket engine nozzle to spec and building the electronics test computer that fires it, as part of BURPG's intro pipeline. The nozzle has to interface with a fixed aluminum adapter and chamber using a 220-size O-ring seal and a 1" NPT connection, sized against real design parameters — chamber pressure, mass flow rate, and combustion gas properties — rather than arbitrary numbers.

SolidWorks FEA Nozzle Design Arduino Instrumentation O-Ring Sealing

Where it stands

Currently in the design review phase, working through nozzle calculations (specific impulse, thrust, exit velocity, area ratio) and drafting the CAD to mate with the fixed adapter geometry. The test computer side needs to read a load cell, fire an EMatch, and control a solenoid — all through a microcontroller I'm building from scratch. Full FEA validation and test firing come later in the semester.
