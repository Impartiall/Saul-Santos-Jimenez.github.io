About

I'm a first-generation college student from Lawrence, MA studying Mechanical Engineering at Boston University. I came into this field because I wanted to understand how things are actually built — not just designed.

My day-to-day at EPIC has me running CNC mills and lathes, the waterjet, wire EDM, laser cutters, and sheet metal tools, and coaching students and researchers on safe setup. I'm learning what it takes to go from a CAD model to a precise physical object, and I'm getting better at it.

Outside of engineering I shoot photo and video — I've directed and edited for a school TV channel and taught photography to younger students. Heritage Spanish speaker.

EK131 · Spring 2026 Finished

Arduino Temperature Monitor

A battery-powered, standalone room temperature monitor built from scratch over one semester. A TMP36 analog sensor feeds the Arduino's 10-bit ADC, and an I2C LCD shows real-time temperature in Fahrenheit. A buzzer and blinking red LED alarm trigger whenever temperature leaves the 62–75°F range. I modeled the enclosure in Onshape, 3D printed a flexible battery holder, and soldered the wiring by hand.

Current draw45–50 mA
Battery life~10–11 hr
Supply9 V
Alarm band62–75°F
Arduino Uno C++ Onshape CAD 3D Printing KVL Soldering 22 AWG wiring

What I learned

The gap between a circuit on paper and one that works reliably inside an enclosure is where most of the real engineering happens. Tolerances, wire routing, heat shrink insulation, and troubleshooting short circuits taught me more than any single lecture did.

EPIC Lab · Summer 2026 Finished

D-Shaft Coupler for ME359

Designed and machined a D-shaft coupler on the wire EDM at EPIC for ME359, a new hands-on transmission course being developed at BU to replace ME358. The part needed a transition fit to seat on the shaft without play — a topic I hadn't covered in coursework yet, so I learned it on my own, modeled the coupler in Onshape, and dialed in a 0.003" offset to land the fit.

Wire EDM Onshape CAD Transition fit Precision tolerancing

What I learned

How to teach myself a fit I'd never used, then commit to it on one of the most precise machines in the shop. Getting a transition fit right means the tolerance call you make in CAD has to survive the real cut — there's no fudging it afterward.

BURPG · Fall 2026 In Progress

Hybrid Intro Project — Rocket Nozzle

Designing a hybrid rocket nozzle to real combustion parameters for BURPG's intro pipeline: 80 psia chamber pressure, 3250 K, 13 g/s mass flow. I sized the throat and exit from those numbers, compared a cone, a 60% bell, and an 80% bell, and chose the 80% bell for the best performance-to-length trade. It's modeled in SolidWorks with an equation-driven bell contour and mates to a supplied adapter flange with a 220-size O-ring seal and a 1" NPT chamber connection. The nozzle prints in ABS resin — the right call for an intro project given the complex contour and low cost, with the understanding that it only needs to survive a few seconds of fire.

Throat dia.0.284 in
Exit dia.0.726 in
Area ratio6.52
Design thrust~35 N
Contour80% bell
Mass (resin)~0.026 lb
SolidWorks Nozzle Design Equation-driven contour O-Ring Sealing 3D Printing (ABS resin)

Where it stands

The initial CAD is finished and heading into a preliminary design review. Next comes FEA to find where the nozzle needs more material — most likely the wall near the throat — then printing and a test fire in the coming weeks. A clip of that fire goes in the slot below once it's recorded.

BURPG · Jan 2026–Present Leadership

Machining Intro Project — Lead

I lead BURPG's Machining Intro Project, the team's onboarding path for hands-on manufacturing, serving 100+ interested members. I organize the stock (6061-T6 aluminum and HDPE), sign-ups, and an educator meeting so instructors teach consistently. New members machine a two-piece machinist's hammer — a 6061-T6 aluminum handle on the manual lathe and an HDPE head on the manual mill — ending in a 3/8"-16 threaded interface they cut and fit themselves. I run the machining hours at EPIC, walking members through lathe and mill setups, DRO use, tolerancing, and safe shop practice.

Manual Lathe Manual Mill 6061-T6 Aluminum HDPE Threading / Tapping Instruction

What I learned

Teaching a process is the fastest way to find the gaps in your own understanding. Getting new members safe and confident on the machines — and keeping the project running week to week — is a different skill than machining a part myself, and one I've grown into.
